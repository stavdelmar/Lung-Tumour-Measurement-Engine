LungMarker: results summary

Research use only. Not for clinical use. No clinical claims. AI-assisted finding, measuring and matching of lung nodules on CT, built for the Harbor Thoracic Oncology Lab (simulated). Tested on public data (RIDER Lung CT); not yet on the lab's own scans. Status 19 Oct 2026. Full version: RESULTS.md in the repository.
1 · Time
Estimate: the tool's own mechanics take about 1–2 minutes per scan; the reader's judgement time with the tool is not measured yet, so the time saved is still a range.
  • Today, by Daniel's own account, a follow-up scan takes 40–55 min by hand (two-thirds of incoming scans are follow-ups).
  • Developer pilot, 7 scans: opening, confirming, editing, matching and export took 1–2 active min. That is overhead, not reading time.
  • With the tool, a follow-up takes that overhead plus Daniel's checking of each AI draft: somewhere between a few minutes and close to today's time. Whether the lab's target (about half) is met can't be shown yet.
2 · Quality
On 52 tumours from 28 patients, the AI's first draft is close to the expert on average but not as precise per tumour, so every value is checked and confirmed before it is recorded.
  • Expert-marked tumour found in 52 of 59 scans (88%); matched to the earlier scan in 24 of 24 pairs.
  • Long axis: median error 3.0 mm, no systematic bias; 20 of 52 within the expert's own scan-to-scan variation (2.3 mm). Volume: median error 14%; 27 of 52 within 17%.
3 · Failures
The AI misses some central tumours and draws some outlines far off; the app makes the reviewer catch these, and some cases are untested.
  • All 7 misses (4 of 31 patients) are central tumours touching the hilum or mediastinum, so the reviewer must check those areas before approving.
  • 19 of 52 long axes are more than 5 mm off the expert. Not tested yet: faint ground-glass nodules, false alarms, the lab's own scans.

How time was measured

Why an estimate. The real test is Daniel timing his own work with and without the tool on the lab's scans. That isn't possible yet: the scans never leave the hospital and the tool runs on public data. So, as agreed with Base44's FDE lead (Sam, 18 Oct), we combine Daniel's step-by-step account of his manual review with the developer's timed runs in the tool, and label everything as an estimate.

How to read the range. Low end: the mechanics alone (~2 min), not a realistic reading time. High end: today's time minus recording (~35–52 min), as if checking each AI draft took as long as doing the step by hand; the new steps could push it higher. We report no single "with the tool" figure and no percentage saved: these data can't support one. Main caveats: the developer is not a trained reader, and the public scans have larger, clearer tumours than the lab's.

Quality against the expert

Test set. RIDER Lung CT (public): 31 patients scanned twice, 15 minutes apart; 59 scans with an expert outline of one tumour each. The pipeline ran with no hint. Every measurement is taken the way Daniel takes it (long axis on the slice where the tumour is largest, short axis at right angles, volume from the outline), on the AI's outline and on the expert's.

Limits

  • Not tested: faint ground-glass nodules (none in the test data; Daniel's hardest cases), false alarms (the data marks one tumour per scan; only a proxy: 82% of AI marks reappear on the repeat scan), and the lab's own scans.
  • Public practice data: mostly large, solid tumours; the lab's cohort may differ.
  • First draft only: we measured the AI's draft, not the reviewer's confirmed values.
  • Same tumours for tuning and testing: outline settings were compared on the 52 test tumours, so quality numbers are somewhat optimistic.
  • Follow-up = same-day repeat scan: tests matching, not growth.
  • Timing is an estimate: small samples (7 runs, a handful of log rows), a developer rather than a reader, and the reader's judgement time not measured.

Next steps

  • Measure the reader's judgement time: Daniel times his own reviews with and without the tool on a small set of the lab's scans, in a hospital pilot with the engine on one hospital server. This is the only way to turn the timing range into a number, and it also measures the quality of his confirmed values.
  • Ground-glass test on the public LNDb set (27 ground-glass + 46 part-solid nodules ≥ 4 mm).
  • Central tumours: try a second model trained on lung-cancer outlines; keep the manual central check until a test shows it works.
  • Follow-up view: export the lung alignment from the engine, so matching checks and the side-by-side view use it.
  • Cost for a research budget (last year's tool failed on price): write down the full yearly cost before any rollout, namely the Base44 plan, one hospital server (an existing machine if it has a GPU or at least 8 GB of memory) and the IT hours, so the lab can compare it with last year's tool. No licence or per-scan fees for the AI models.

Appendix B · Daniel's manual baseline (his words, Slack, 18 Oct)

Per follow-up scan: loading 1–2 min; re-finding and matching ~10 min per nodule (aligns by bronchi and fissures; slice numbers are unreliable because of breathing); finding new nodules 10–20 min (two passes over 250–400 slices); measuring 2–4 min per nodule; full outline 15–25 min per tumour when needed; recording 3–5 min; total 40–55 min. His log ("Minutes on scan", last 5 rows): baseline 38, 49, 30, 37; follow-up 55. About two-thirds of incoming scans are follow-ups (his estimate).

Appendix C · Technical method

  • Finding: TotalSegmentator lung_nodules (nnU-Net, ~1,350 training CTs partly from LIDC-IDRI, not RIDER; Apache-2.0) at full settings, on a free Colab T4 GPU (median 49 s per scan). Lobes: TotalSegmentator total.
  • Outline: the model's core plus a rim scaled to the core's size (shipped setting). Compared first with the model's own outline and MedSAM 2; four rim variants compared on the 52 tumours (halo_variants_found52.csv).
  • Measurements: long axis = longest diameter on the slice where it is largest; short axis = longest perpendicular on that slice; volume from the 3D outline; lobe = majority lobe.
  • Matching: affine lung registration (SimpleITK) + Hungarian pairing, gate max(10 mm, 0.75 × long axis).
  • Expert yardstick: median absolute difference between the expert's manual outlines on the two scans of each patient (28 patients).
  • "AI unsure about the edges": mean model probability over the outline < 0.6, or the rim would grow the long axis by ≥ 4 mm.
