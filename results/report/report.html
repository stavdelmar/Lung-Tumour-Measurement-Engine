LungMarker: results summary

Research use only. Not for clinical use. No clinical claims. LungMarker helps a researcher measure lung tumours on CT scans faster. An AI finds the tumours and drafts the measurements; the researcher checks and corrects each one, and the results go straight into the lab's Excel sheet. Built in 7 days for the Harbor Thoracic Oncology Lab (simulated), tested on a public set of CT scans. 19 Oct 2026.

The app picks up new scans by itself. In real use, step 2 would run on one server inside the hospital, because real scans can't leave it.

How close is the AI to an expert?

We ran the AI on a public set of 59 CT scans (31 patients, each scanned twice 15 minutes apart), where an expert had outlined one tumour per scan, and measured both outlines the way Daniel measures.

  • Finding: the tumour was found in 52 of 59 scans (88%), and matched to the same patient's other scan in 24 of 24 cases.
  • Measuring: the AI's longest-width measurement is typically about 3 mm from the expert's, with no tendency to over- or under-measure (52 tumours, 28 patients). For comparison, the expert's own two measurements of the same tumour differ by about 2.3 mm. The AI is within that range for 20 of 52 tumours, so it's a good first draft, not a final answer.
  • Volume: typically 14% from the expert; within the expert's own variation (17%) for 27 of 52.

What the researcher gets

  • Excel in the lab's format: the same 18 columns, order and formats as Daniel's sheet; only new rows each day; a second sheet logs what the AI drafted and what he changed. Checked on real exports and by 30 automated checks.
  • Review that respects his judgement: accept or reject each AI suggestion, fix outlines by hand, add anything it missed. Nothing is saved until he confirms.
  • Organised by patient: a patient's scans are grouped, and the earlier scan opens next to the new one.

What doesn't work (yet), and how I would fix it

Central tumours missed
7 of 59 scans (4 of 31 patients). Every miss was in the centre of the chest, where the lungs meet the heart and main airways, including large tumours (14–52 mm). The lab says these are common in its patients.
Now: the app won't let a scan be approved until the researcher confirms they checked that area. An automatic warning was tried and dropped: it flagged almost every scan.
Some outlines far off
19 of 52 AI outlines were more than 5 mm off the expert's (13 too small, 6 too large).
Now: nothing is saved until the researcher confirms the outline; some likely-too-small outlines are flagged "AI unsure about the edges".
No real usability test
The lab's users are simulated chat personas: they answer questions but can't open links or use the tool. We used screenshots and one external tester instead.
Impact: the time saved is an estimate, and ease of use is untested with the real user.
Not yet tested
Faint "ground-glass" nodules: none in the practice scans; the open set that has them (LNDb) needs approval that takes up to 9 days, after the deadline, and we chose not to use it before approval. Also untested: false alarms (the data marks one tumour per scan), the lab's own scans. Small nodules' volume varies ~30% between repeat scans.
Base44 limits
The builder was strong at setting up the app's structure and data, weak at fine screen detail; the GitHub sync → Publish order caused confusing test results. Much of the work moved to code.
Not a real deployment
The AI runs in batches on a free cloud GPU. In a hospital it needs one server inside the network, a data-privacy review and a written yearly cost.

How I would fix the central-tumour miss

The AI model itself sees almost nothing at those spots, and three of the four missed tumours grow along blood vessels, so a simple rule can't tell them apart from normal vessels. It needs a different model, not a setting.

  • A stronger computer with a GPU. Everything ran on free tools this week. With a GPU (or paid cloud GPU time), I would run a second AI model trained on lung-cancer scans that include central tumours, and use it only where it disagrees with the current one.
  • A bigger test. We had only 4 patients with central tumours, too few to judge any fix. Next: a larger public set, then a sample of the lab's own scans inside the hospital, counting misses and false alarms.
  • A paid model, carefully. Commercial tools exist but weren't evaluated, and price is why the lab dropped last year's tool. Only if a test shows it catches central tumours and the yearly cost fits a research budget.
  • Until then, the required manual check of the centre of the chest stays.

How I would run the usability test with real users

  • Watch, don't help: give Daniel a real task ("a follow-up scan for patient X came in, record it as you would for the study"), ask him to think aloud, don't explain, then talk it through; rank problems by how serious and how hard to fix.
  • Time it properly: Daniel times his own work on a set of the lab's scans, with and without the tool, the same way both times.
  • Check the final numbers: compare the values he confirms with the tool against his manual ones on the same scans.

Other limits: the public scans have mostly large, clear tumours (13–93 mm; the lab measures from 4 mm); we measured the AI's first draft, not the researcher's corrected values; settings were tuned on the same tumours, so the numbers are a little optimistic.

Appendix B · Daniel's manual baseline (his words, Slack, 18 Oct)

Per follow-up scan: loading 1–2 min; re-finding and matching ~10 min per nodule (aligns by bronchi and fissures; slice numbers are unreliable because of breathing); finding new nodules 10–20 min (two passes over 250–400 slices); measuring 2–4 min per nodule; full outline 15–25 min per tumour when needed; recording 3–5 min; total 40–55 min. His log ("Minutes on scan", last 5 rows): baseline 38, 49, 30, 37; follow-up 55. About two-thirds of incoming scans are follow-ups (his estimate).

Appendix C · Technical method

  • Finding: TotalSegmentator lung_nodules (nnU-Net, ~1,350 training CTs partly from LIDC-IDRI, not RIDER; Apache-2.0) at full settings, on a free Colab T4 GPU (median 49 s per scan). Lobes: TotalSegmentator total.
  • Outline: the model's core plus a rim scaled to the core's size (shipped setting). Compared first with the model's own outline and MedSAM 2; four rim variants compared on the 52 tumours (halo_variants_found52.csv).
  • Measurements: long axis = longest diameter on the slice where it is largest; short axis = longest perpendicular on that slice; volume from the 3D outline; lobe = majority lobe.
  • Matching: affine lung registration (SimpleITK) + Hungarian pairing, gate max(10 mm, 0.75 × long axis).
  • Expert yardstick: median absolute difference between the expert's manual outlines on the two scans of each patient (28 patients).
  • "AI unsure about the edges": mean model probability over the outline < 0.6, or the rim would grow the long axis by ≥ 4 mm.
